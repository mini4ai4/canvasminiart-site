What size wall art above a sofa or bed?

The most common regret people write to me about is not the colour and not the frame. It is that they bought too small.

A print that looked generous on a screen turns into a postage stamp once it is on a wall. It happens because we judge size against the picture on our phone, not against the furniture it will hang over. Here is the rule I give when someone sends me a photo of their wall.

The rule: two thirds

Art above a piece of furniture should be roughly two thirds the width of that furniture. Not half. Two thirds.

A standard three-seat sofa is about 84 inches (215 cm) wide. Two thirds of that is around 56 inches (142 cm). That can be one large piece, or two or three prints hung together with a small gap between them — the gap counts as part of the group.

How high to hang it

Centre of the artwork at 57–60 inches (145–152 cm) from the floor. That is average eye level, and it is the height galleries use. Above furniture, leave 6–10 inches (15–25 cm) between the top of the sofa back and the bottom of the frame — closer than that looks cramped, further and the art floats away from the furniture.

Test it before you buy. Cut the size out of newspaper or wrapping paper and tape it to the wall. Live with it for a day. This costs nothing and settles the argument better than any measurement — and it is how you discover that the size you were about to order is one step too small.

When to go bigger than the rule

  • High ceilings. Above about 9 feet (2.7 m), art needs more scale or it disappears.
  • Minimal rooms. The emptier the wall, the larger the piece can be. In a room with one focal point, one big piece beats three small ones.
  • Black and white art. Monochrome reads visually lighter than colour, so it can be sized up without dominating.

When to go smaller

  • Narrow hallways, where you never see the piece from more than a metre away.
  • Walls already busy with shelves, mirrors or windows.
  • Rented walls where you are limited to one small hook.
