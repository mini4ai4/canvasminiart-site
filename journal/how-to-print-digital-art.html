How to print a digital download so it actually looks good

A printable file is cheap because you do one step yourself. That step is not complicated, but two small mistakes account for almost every disappointing result.

If you have just bought a printable file — from me or anyone else — this is what to do with it.

1. Check what you have

Open the file and look at two things: the size it is made for, and the resolution. A file intended for large prints is usually supplied at 300 DPI, which means it holds enough detail to print big without going soft. My files are prepared for large formats, so printing them small is always safe; the risk runs the other way.

2. Choose the size before you choose the shop

Decide the size first, because it changes where you print. Anything up to A4 or 8×10 in you can do at home. Bigger than that and you want a shop.

If you are unsure what size the wall needs, read the sizing guide first — the most common regret is going one size too small.

4. Ask for the right paper

For black and white ink work, ask for matte or fine-art matte paper, 200 gsm or heavier. Matte holds the softness of ink washes and does not throw glare under a lamp. Glossy paper makes ink art look like a photograph of a painting rather than the thing itself.

The two mistakes. First: printing “fit to page” without checking, which quietly scales the image and leaves uneven margins. Choose actual size or 100%, or specify the exact print size at the shop. Second: choosing glossy because it sounds premium. For ink art it is the wrong surface.

5. Framing without custom costs

Print to a standard size and an off-the-shelf frame fits. That is the whole trick to keeping framing cheap. If your print is slightly smaller than the frame, use a mat — it costs little and makes a modest print look considered.

6. If it comes out wrong

Too dark is the usual complaint, and it is almost always the printer, not the file. Ask the shop to print a small test at the same settings before running the full size. Any decent shop will do this and it costs a dollar.

Why buy a printable at all

  • You choose the size, and you can reprint bigger later from the same file.
  • No shipping, no waiting, no customs.
  • It is the cheapest way to see whether a piece works on your wall before committing to a large framed version.

That last point is the honest one. If you are not sure a piece suits the room, a printable is a low-risk way to find out.
